# OPP-0004 — Liquidation-cascade reversal (forced selling exhaustion)

- rank: **INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE** · evidence score: **13/50** · status: `NOT_SELECTED_DATA_INTEGRITY_AND_SAMPLE`
- sources: S-12, S-13
- market: Binance spot BTC and major alts (execution) with liquidation events (signal)
- assets: BTC, major crypto
- timeframe: minutes to hours

## Economic hypothesis

Liquidation cascades are forced, price-insensitive selling: each forced close pushes price further, triggering the next. Once the forced flow exhausts, the price impact has no fundamental content and should partially revert, so buying into the exhaustion of a documented cascade should earn the reversal.

## Counter-evidence

- S-13: Binance and Bybit throttled their public liquidation WebSocket feeds to about one liquidation per second, so full cascade size cannot be reconstructed from the public feed; any measurement of 'cascade size' from it is a lower bound of unknown tightness.
- Cascades are rare: even a five-year sample yields few independent episodes, which is the same power problem the project already met with its 2-sigma volatility-spike line (6 trades).
- Catching the exhaustion live requires entering while the market is still moving; the realised fill is worse than the minute close used in any event study.
- The project has already tested the neighbouring family (long a 2-sigma daily realised-vol down-spike: INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE, 6 trades; fade a >=3-day >=2xATR void: REJECT_VALIDATION). A cascade-reversal rule risks being the same family with renamed rules, which the reentry policy forbids.

## Ambiguities

- Cascade definition (drawdown size, volume surge, liquidation notional, time window) is not specified by any source.
- Whether the reversal is tradeable on spot (long after the flush) or only on derivatives is unresolved.
- Binance's own liquidation prints are censored (S-13), so the source's data basis may not be reproducible at all.
