# OPP-0001 — Spot-BTC ETF net-flow continuation (structural creation demand)

- rank: **RESEARCH_HYPOTHESIS_P0** (reclassified from `REPLICATE_P0` by RFC-035E) · evidence score: **19/50** · status: `RESEARCH_HYPOTHESIS_SELECTED_FOR_VALIDATION`
- validation: **EFFECT_NOT_REPRODUCED** (RFC-035E, independent verification PASS 107/107) -> this candidate is rejected
- sources: S-01, S-02, S-03, S-04
- market: Binance spot BTC/USDT (execution) vs US spot Bitcoin ETF daily net flows (signal)
- assets: BTC
- timeframe: daily signal, holding horizon 1-5 days (S-01 impulse peaks around days 3-4)

## Economic hypothesis

US spot Bitcoin ETF creations are in-kind or cash purchases of spot BTC executed by authorised participants. Net creations therefore represent mechanical, price-insensitive spot demand that is visible with a lag and is persistent, while prices are already informative about it. If previous-day net flows carry information about the next days' spot price, a long/cash spot position conditioned on the sign of net flows should capture part of that demand.

## Rules as stated by the source (nothing invented)

- entry: UNKNOWN (no source in S-01..S-04 states a rule). The only rule-shaped statement found is the estimated VAR relation: previous-day ETF net inflow has a positive coefficient on today's BTC return (0.027). Candidate operational form to be specified in RFC-036, not here.
- exit: UNKNOWN (not stated by any source).
- risk: UNKNOWN as a source rule. Project constraints that any future spec must satisfy: long/cash only, no derivatives, 200 USDT reference capital, 23.791 bps round-trip cost floor.
- parameters: UNKNOWN (no thresholds, lookbacks or sizing are given). S-01 uses 7-day aggregation to reduce noise and an orthogonalised impulse response over 10 days; it does not define a tradable parameter set.

## Claimed evidence

- period: 2024-01-11 to 2024-10-11 (S-01)
- metrics: correlation 0.30 between flow changes and price changes; Granger causality F=8.4767, p=0.00406; VAR coefficient of previous-day flow on today's return 0.027; flow persistence 0.533; price momentum 0.802; impulse response peak about +1.2% at days 3-4, non-negative
- transaction cost model: NONE in the source. The project's own floor for any replication is 23.791 bps round trip (Binance spot standard taker, as fixed in the internal cost model).
- external code: NONE (no repository or script published with S-01..S-04)
- external results: S-01: statistically significant Granger causality from flows to BTC price, persistent positive impulse response peaking near +1.2%. S-02/S-03/S-04: peer-reviewed price-discovery/return-volatility effects around the ETF market, i.e. the ETF channel is materially connected to spot price formation.
- forward evidence: NONE · live evidence: NONE

## Required data

daily US spot BTC ETF net flows (public tables, several vendors, reconstructible historically); BTC/USDT daily and 1h OHLCV from Binance; publication timing of flows relative to Binance trading (PIT requirement)

## Counter-evidence

- S-01 itself: correlation is only 0.30, so less than 10% of price variation is explained; the relation is unstable (rolling 60-day correlation 0.10-0.50).
- S-01 itself: a feedback loop from price to flows means part of the flow signal is a consequence of the price move already realised.
- S-01 itself: short sample (10 months at publication), stationarity assumed, non-linearity possible.
- The project's own cost floor (23.791 bps round trip) is a large share of the 1.2% shock response, so the post-cost remainder depends heavily on how many independent shocks exist.
- Publication timing: daily flow data is published after the US close, so the tradable part is the residual move after the print — exactly the part the source's causality test says is smaller.
- The source is buy-side research, not peer-reviewed, and it evaluates a statistical relation, not a strategy with fees, slippage and turnover.

## Ambiguities

- No entry/exit rule, threshold, lookback or sizing is defined by any source: an RFC-036 spec must fix them before looking at data (and must declare that the choices are ours, not the source's).
- Whether flows should be used as a level, a change, a normalised difference, a 7-day aggregate or a sign is not settled by the sources.
- The exact PIT publication timestamp per day is vendor-specific and must be pinned before replication.

## Risk classification

- replication difficulty: LOW-MEDIUM: data is free and shallow (one series + one price series), but the rule set must be frozen by us and the honest sample is short (about 21 months of ETF history, far fewer independent episodes).
- evidence quality: MIXED: peer-reviewed papers (S-02..S-04) establish the ETF-spot linkage but not tradability; the only predictability claim with numbers (S-01) is industry research with a declared weak correlation.
- selection bias: MEDIUM: ETF-flow strategies were widely publicised during 2024-2025; any rule we now invent is chosen with knowledge of that period. · overfitting: MEDIUM-HIGH: a flexible rule set (threshold, lookback, aggregation) on ~640 daily observations can be fitted easily. RFC-036 must pre-register choices. · lookahead: MEDIUM: flows are published with a lag, but several free tables retro-fill intraday revisions; the replication must pin a single publication timestamp per day. · implementation: LOW: a long/cash BTC position is expressible in the existing runtime; no derivatives, no shorting, no borrow.
