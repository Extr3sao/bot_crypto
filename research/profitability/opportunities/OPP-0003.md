# OPP-0003 — USDT net inflows to exchanges predict BTC/ETH returns

- rank: **WATCH** · evidence score: **17/50** · status: `BLOCKED_ON_DATA_AND_COST_HEADROOM`
- sources: S-07, S-17, S-16
- market: BTC and ETH spot (execution) vs on-chain USDT exchange flows (signal)
- assets: BTC, ETH, USDT
- timeframe: 1-2 hours for returns, 6 hours for volatility

## Economic hypothesis

Stablecoins are the settlement layer of crypto trading. When USDT is minted and moved onto exchanges, buying power enters the venue order books; the flow is observable on-chain before it is fully reflected in price. Hence net exchange inflows should predict short-horizon returns and be followed by lower volatility as the buying is absorbed.

## Required data

labelled USDT exchange-flow series (on-chain); BTC/ETH hourly OHLCV; exchange and address labels. Commercial providers dominate this data layer.

## Counter-evidence

- S-16: forward evidence that stablecoin flows/depegs predict crypto returns is weak and does not cohere into a systematic pattern.
- Requiring a signal at a 1-2 hour horizon collides with the project runtime's 15-minute cycle and its 23.791 bps round-trip cost floor: the effect size in bps is not given at the level read, and typical 1-hour crypto returns are far smaller than the cost floor.
- Data-access risk: labelled exchange-flow series are typically commercial; a free substitute would be a different (unvalidated) construction - a silent fidelity change.
- Mint events are partly endogenous: issuers mint because demand exists, and the demand itself is price-driven.

## Ambiguities

- The exact flow construction (mint vs transfer-to-exchange vs net exchange balance change) is not specified at the level read.
- Whether the effect survives exchange-level aggregation (Binance only) is not established.
- Publication/computation timestamps of on-chain flow series are not pinned.
