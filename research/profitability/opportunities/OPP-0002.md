# OPP-0002 — Token unlock / vesting supply events (scheduled forced supply)

- rank: **RESEARCH_HYPOTHESIS_P1** (reclassified from `REPLICATE_P1` by RFC-035E) · evidence score: **22/50** · status: `NOT_SELECTED_DIRECTION_AND_FIDELITY`
- sources: S-09, S-10, S-11
- market: Binance spot altcoins with published vesting schedules
- assets: altcoins with cliff unlocks, SOL, APE, OP as examples in S-09
- timeframe: 30 days before to ~30 days after an unlock event

## Economic hypothesis

Vesting cliffs release supply to recipients (team, investors, ecosystem) whose cost basis is far below market. The release date is known in advance, so recipients and everyone else can front-run the liquidity; price pressure therefore starts before the cliff and continues after it, until the released supply is absorbed.

## Rules as stated by the source (nothing invented)

- entry: UNKNOWN (S-09 analyses event windows and groups events; it does not publish an entry rule). Its usable rule-shaped statements are descriptive: pressure begins about 30 days before the event; bigger unlocks fall about 2.4x more sharply; team unlocks about -25%.
- exit: UNKNOWN (not stated).
- risk: UNKNOWN as a source rule. Note that the documented direction is negative, which a long/cash spot runtime cannot express directly.
- parameters: S-09 size bands are explicit: nano <0.1%, micro 0.1-0.5%, small 0.5-1%, medium 1-5% of supply, plus recipient classes (team, investors, ecosystem). No tradable thresholds beyond that.

## Claimed evidence

- period: UNKNOWN (multi-year, not stated)
- metrics: 16,000+ unlock events analysed; 90% of unlocks negative; impact starts ~30 days pre-event; larger unlocks about 2.4x sharper drops; team unlocks about -25%; ecosystem-development unlocks +1.18% average
- transaction cost model: NONE. S-09 does not model fees or slippage, and the thin-float tokens it targets are exactly where slippage is worst.
- external code: NONE
- external results: S-09 (vendor, 16k events) reports a broad negative effect with recipient-type and size conditioning. S-11 (2026 working paper, 52 events) reports a preliminary 72-hour post-unlock effect. S-10 (236 events) is the counter-study: effects are conditional and mostly priced in before the unlock.
- forward evidence: NONE · live evidence: NONE

## Required data

historical vesting/unlock calendars per token (public via several providers, partly paid); spot OHLCV; token float/supply to size events relative to circulating supply; listing dates for the early-stage filter (see S-10)

## Counter-evidence

- S-10: unlock drops are concentrated in early-stage, thin-float tokens and are mostly priced in before the event - i.e. the residual, tradable part is the smallest part.
- S-09's own headline is drawn from a dataset that is not published; only summary statistics are available, so the sample cannot be re-derived exactly.
- Direction problem: the documented effect is negative, so expressing it requires shorting or avoiding. A long/cash runtime can only avoid, and avoidance has no value without a base long strategy that would otherwise hold the token.
- Slippage: the tokens where the effect is largest are the least liquid; the project's 23.791 bps round trip is not a plausible cost for them.
- Vesting calendars are periodically renegotiated/accelerated, so an ex-ante calendar can differ from the realised unlock.

## Ambiguities

- No entry/exit rule exists in any source; whether the tradable object is 'avoid the window' or 'buy the post-event dip' is unresolved.
- Event classification ('team' vs 'investor' vs 'ecosystem') varies by provider and is not standardised.
- The horizon (30 days pre; 72 hours post) differs between sources and is not reconciled.

## Risk classification

- replication difficulty: MEDIUM-HIGH: the rules must be invented, the full 16k-event sample is unavailable, and the long-only expression is unresolved.
- evidence quality: MIXED-LOW for tradability: large-sample vendor research plus one small academic working paper, with a vendor counter-study that undercuts the tradable residual.
- selection bias: HIGH: tokens are chosen and calendars are only published for tokens that grew enough to matter. · overfitting: MEDIUM-HIGH: event windows and size bands are freely parameterisable. · lookahead: LOW-MEDIUM: dates are pre-scheduled (good), but calendars are often revised and some are published retroactively. · implementation: MEDIUM: the asset universe of the runtime is narrow; thin-float tokens may not be tradable within it.
